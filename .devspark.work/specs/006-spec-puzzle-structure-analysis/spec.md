# Feature Specification: Puzzle Structure, Challenge, and Character

**Feature Branch**: `006-spec-puzzle-structure-analysis`
**Created**: 2026-09-27
**Status**: Complete
**Input**: Build a small experimental laboratory inside ArrowSpark's existing architecture: a headless structural-analysis capability over `PuzzleDefinition` (board scale, arrow geometry, legal-move structure, a dependency graph, cascade/unlock-fan-out, blocker-distance proxies), six deliberately different new experimental authored puzzles that push the existing rules harder than the first eight, an extended catalog regression gate, a developer-facing comparison report, and a lightweight human-calibration worksheet — explicitly to gather evidence about what makes an ArrowSpark puzzle challenging, satisfying, and recognizable before any procedural generation is attempted. No rule changes. No generator. No player-facing difficulty labels or composite score.

## Product Owner TLDR

ArrowSpark's eight shipped puzzles all work, but a dedicated research pass found they're easy for a specific, provable reason: each one isolates exactly one structural feature (a bend, a chain, a wide branch, or raw density) and none combine two. The game's removal rule is also provably "safe" — no legal move can ever backfire — so real challenge here is about *noticing* things, not *choosing* between risky options. Before we teach a generator to build ArrowSpark puzzles, we need evidence about what actually makes noticing hard and rewarding. This spec adds a measurement toolkit for puzzle structure, six new hand-built puzzles that each deliberately combine features the current catalog never does (including the untested "cascade" moment where clearing one arrow unlocks several), a way for a developer to compare puzzles side by side, and a two-minute-per-puzzle worksheet to check whether the numbers match how a puzzle actually feels to play. Nothing here changes the game a player sees today beyond six more puzzles to play.

## Rationale Summary

### Core Problem

The eight catalog puzzles (spec 005) are solver-validated and playable, but human playtesting confirmed they're "fairly basic and easy to solve." A pre-spec research pass (grounded directly in `PuzzleDefinition`/`PuzzleState`/`PuzzleSolver` and a re-implementation of the solver's own walk against all eight puzzles) proved *why*: ArrowSpark's removal rule is monotonic, so a legal move can never re-block another arrow or turn a solvable puzzle unsolvable. Strategic difficulty (a choice that can go wrong) is therefore structurally impossible under the current rules — every puzzle's challenge is perceptual (finding a legal arrow, tracing a bent tail, spotting a distant blocker). The research also found the eight puzzles under-use even that perceptual space: only 3 of 8 use any bent arrow, none combines density with real dependency depth, and no puzzle anywhere in the catalog has an arrow whose removal unlocks more than one other arrow (a "cascade"). We do not yet have validated evidence about which of these structural features actually produce a satisfying play experience, and building a puzzle generator before that evidence exists risks teaching it to optimize for the wrong thing.

### Decision Summary

Add a headless, deterministic structural-analysis capability that measures objective characteristics of any `PuzzleDefinition` (scale, density, arrow geometry, legal-move structure, a dependency graph, cascade/unlock fan-out, blocker-distance proxies) without touching gameplay rules or presentation; author six new experimental puzzles that each deliberately combine structural features the current catalog leaves untested; extend the existing automated content-regression gate to cover all fourteen puzzles; add a lightweight developer report for comparing puzzles; and add a short manual calibration worksheet to check the measurements against real play experience. Explicitly do not build a difficulty formula, player-facing rating, or generator in this spec.

### Key Drivers

- Existing human playtesting already told us the current content is too easy; we need evidence-driven next steps, not another guess.
- The removal rule's monotonicity is now a proven structural fact, not a hypothesis — it reframes "difficulty" work around perception rather than strategy for as long as the rule stays unchanged.
- The pre-spec research identified concrete, previously-untested combinations (cascade, density+dependency, multi-bend interaction, long-range blocking, composition) that are achievable today with zero rule changes.
- A future puzzle-generation spec needs a validated model of what makes a puzzle interesting; generating puzzles before that model exists risks wasted generator-design effort.

### Tradeoffs Considered

- Building a difficulty formula or composite score now: rejected — the research explicitly found we have one human data point ("the eight puzzles are easy") and no validated model connecting structural metrics to perceived difficulty; a formula built now would encode an unvalidated guess as if it were fact.
- Building a procedural generator now: rejected — every generation approach the research compared (random+validate, reverse construction, dependency-first, mutation, search/evolutionary, hybrid) either has low interestingness control or depends on a validated objective function that doesn't exist yet; generation is deferred to a later spec by explicit user direction.
- Extending `PuzzleSolver` itself to hold every new metric: rejected in favor of a clearly-scoped sibling analysis component (exact shape left to planning) — the request explicitly warns against turning the solver into "a miscellaneous statistics container," and solver correctness must stay decoupled from speculative difficulty concepts.
- Automating "dependency discoverability," "false affordance," or a composite "unlock rhythm" score: rejected — these remain explicitly-labeled human-perception hypotheses to be checked by the calibration worksheet, not objective metrics to compute and trust.
- Treating the six new puzzles as "Levels 9–14, harder than before": rejected — each is framed as a named experiment isolating a specific structural hypothesis (nested chain, cascade, dense+dependency, bent network, long-range blocker, composed/shaped), not a difficulty progression.

### Architectural Impact

A new analysis capability is introduced between the existing domain layer (`PuzzleDefinition`, `PuzzleState`, `PuzzleSolver`) and any future consumer (developer tooling today; a future generator later). It consumes a `PuzzleDefinition`, may reuse `PuzzleSolver`'s existing witness/metrics, and produces objective structural measurements and a dependency-graph representation — without mutating the definition, without touching `PuzzleState`/gameplay/session state, and without depending on scenes, Nodes, Controls, or any presentation concept. `PuzzleCatalog` grows six new entries (fourteen total); `PuzzleDefinition`'s shape is unchanged. The existing automated content-regression gate is extended to cover the enlarged catalog rather than duplicated. No existing gameplay, scoring, rendering, departure, navigation, or session behavior changes for any of the original eight puzzles or for general play.

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 - Measure any puzzle's objective structure (Priority: P1) ✅ Complete

As an ArrowSpark developer, I want to run structural analysis over any `PuzzleDefinition` and get back objective measurements (board scale, density, arrow geometry, legal-move structure, dependency-graph properties, cascade/unlock fan-out, blocker-distance proxies), so that I can compare puzzles using characteristics that actually exist in the puzzle model rather than guesswork.

**Why this priority**: Every other outcome in this spec — authoring the six experimental puzzles with intent, producing the developer report, and running human calibration against real numbers — depends on this measurement capability existing and being trustworthy first.

**Independent Test**: Feed the analyzer each of the eight existing catalog puzzles plus a handful of tiny synthetic puzzles with hand-computed expected values (a two-arrow independent puzzle, a three-arrow chain, a two-arrow cycle, a puzzle with one arrow blocked by two others, a puzzle where one removal unlocks two arrows at once) and confirm every returned metric matches the expected value exactly, with zero mutation of the input definition.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** any structurally valid, solver-confirmed-solvable `PuzzleDefinition`, **When** it is analyzed, **Then** the analysis returns board scale (width, height, total cells, arrow count, occupied cell count, density), arrow geometry (single-cell/multi-cell counts, average/maximum arrow length, bent-arrow count, total bends, average/maximum bends per arrow), and legal-move structure (initial legal count/ratio, minimum/maximum/average legal choices across the solver's witness, forced-state count/ratio, branching-state count/ratio, longest consecutive forced run, and the full sequence of legal-choice counts state by state).
2. **Given** the same `PuzzleDefinition`, **When** it is analyzed twice in a row, **Then** every returned value is identical both times (deterministic, no hidden randomness or ordering dependency).
3. **Given** any `PuzzleDefinition` passed to the analyzer, **When** analysis completes, **Then** the original definition object is unchanged in every field, no gameplay `PuzzleState` is mutated as a side effect, and no session-scoped current-puzzle state is read or written.
4. **Given** a structurally invalid `PuzzleDefinition`, **When** it is analyzed, **Then** the analysis reports the invalidity plainly (consistent with how `PuzzleSolver.analyze()` already reports `valid: false`) rather than throwing, crashing, or returning misleading nonzero metrics.
5. **Given** a tiny synthetic puzzle whose expected dependency-graph properties are known by hand construction (e.g. a straight three-arrow chain, a two-arrow cycle, one arrow blocked by two others, a single arrow whose removal unlocks exactly two others), **When** it is analyzed, **Then** the reported dependency edges, depth, longest chain, maximum blockers-per-arrow, and maximum unlock fan-out all match the hand-computed expected values exactly.

### User Story 2 - Play six new experiments that combine what the old eight never did (Priority: P1) ✅ Complete

As a player, I want to play new puzzles that feel meaningfully different from the existing eight — puzzles where dependencies are spread across the board, where clearing one arrow visibly opens up several others, where a dense-looking board actually has real structure to unravel, where following bent shapes matters, where something I assumed was free turns out to be blocked from far away, and where the board itself looks like something recognizable — so puzzle 9 onward doesn't just feel like more of the same six patterns I already learned.

**Why this priority**: This is the player-visible half of the spec's evidence-gathering goal; without genuinely varied new content there is nothing for either the analyzer or human calibration to check against.

**Independent Test**: Play each of the six new puzzles start to finish through the existing puzzle scene (no new mechanics, same Level Select/Replay/Next Puzzle flow as the existing eight); confirm each is completable with the existing rules, and confirm via the analyzer's own output that each puzzle's defining structural claim actually holds (e.g. the "cascade" puzzle's analysis shows at least one unlock fan-out of two or more; the "bent network" puzzle's analysis shows multiple bent arrows genuinely participating in dependency edges, not just present decoratively).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the enlarged fourteen-puzzle catalog, **When** a player opens Level Select, **Then** all fourteen puzzles appear in deterministic order, each individually selectable, with the existing eight unchanged in position, identity, and behavior.
2. **Given** the "Nested Chain" experimental puzzle, **When** analyzed, **Then** its dependency graph shows a chain of meaningful depth whose blocking relationships are spread across more than one visually separated board region (not laid out as one obvious straight run), and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
3. **Given** the "Cascade / Key Arrow" experimental puzzle, **When** its solver witness is analyzed step by step, **Then** at least one removal step unlocks two or more previously-blocked arrows simultaneously (a measured cascade/unlock fan-out of at least 2), and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
4. **Given** the "Dense Unravel" experimental puzzle, **When** analyzed, **Then** it shows meaningfully higher occupied-cell density than the original eight's average alongside a nontrivial dependency depth or forced-state ratio (not "dense but 90%+ already legal" like the existing `dense_board` puzzle), and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
5. **Given** the "Bent Network" experimental puzzle, **When** analyzed, **Then** it contains multiple bent arrows and at least one dependency edge whose blocking relationship involves a bent arrow's tail cell (not only head-to-head blocking), and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
6. **Given** the "Long-Range Blocker" experimental puzzle, **When** analyzed, **Then** at least one dependency edge has a measurably large blocker distance (the blocking cell sits well beyond the immediately adjacent cell along the blocked arrow's forward ray) on a board large enough for that distance to be meaningful, and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
7. **Given** the "Composed / Shaped" experimental puzzle, **When** its occupied cells are viewed as a developer-facing grid, **Then** they form a recognizable intentional macro shape while still containing at least one genuine dependency edge among the arrows composing that shape (not a shape whose arrows are all independently and immediately legal), and it remains solver-confirmed solvable with a zero-mistake witness.
8. **Given** all fourteen catalog puzzles, **When** the extended automated content-regression gate runs, **Then** every entry (original eight and six new) has a unique stable ID, is structurally valid, is solver-confirmed solvable, and replays its witness against a fresh `PuzzleState` with zero mistakes.

### User Story 3 - Compare puzzles and record what actually felt interesting (Priority: P2) ✅ Complete

As an ArrowSpark developer, I want a way to see structural differences across the whole catalog at a glance, and a lightweight way to record what a puzzle actually felt like to play, so I can start building an evidence base for what makes a puzzle interesting before designing any generator.

**Why this priority**: This depends on User Stories 1 and 2 already existing (measurements and new content); it's the synthesis step that turns raw numbers and playtesting into usable evidence, but the spec still delivers value without it if time runs short.

**Independent Test**: Run the developer-facing report against the full fourteen-puzzle catalog and confirm it answers, for the whole catalog, which puzzle has the deepest dependency chain, which begins with the fewest legal arrows, which has the widest branching, which produces the largest cascade, which has the longest forced run, which has the highest density, which contains the most bends, which has the longest blocker distance, and which has the largest board — then play each of the six new puzzles once and fill out the calibration worksheet for each.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the full fourteen-puzzle catalog, **When** the developer report is generated, **Then** its output is deterministic (the same catalog produces the same report every run) and lets a developer directly answer each of the nine comparison questions listed above by reading the output, without needing to re-derive them by hand.
2. **Given** one of the six new experimental puzzles has just been played to completion, **When** the calibration worksheet is filled out for it, **Then** it records the puzzle's ID, score, mistakes, and accuracy (already available from existing gameplay results) alongside perceived challenge (1–5), scanning load (low/medium/high), sequence discoverability (obvious / required some tracing / required significant tracing), whether an "aha"/unlock moment occurred (yes/no), whether the puzzle felt solved before it was mechanically complete (yes/no), and a short free-text note on what was interesting (and optionally what was frustrating) about it.
3. **Given** the calibration worksheet process, **When** used across all six new puzzles, **Then** completing it for one puzzle takes on the order of seconds, involves no telemetry, no account, and no persistence infrastructure beyond a plain lightweight document/file.
4. **Given** the developer report and the calibration worksheet together, **When** compared, **Then** a developer can state in plain language where the objective metrics and the human-observed experience agreed or disagreed for at least one of the six new puzzles (a negative finding — a metric that predicted nothing observed in play — counts as a successful outcome of this comparison, not a failure of the spec).
5. **Given** all six calibration worksheets are complete, **When** the findings summary (FR-026) is produced, **Then** it states, for each of the six named experiments, whether the targeted hypothesis was supported, contradicted, or remains inconclusive, and it names at least one structural characteristic worth investigating further before any future puzzle-generation spec — without asserting a difficulty score, formula, or generator design.

### Edge Cases

- Analyzing a structurally invalid `PuzzleDefinition` must behave predictably (report invalidity, return zero/empty metrics) rather than crash or silently produce misleading nonzero output, mirroring `PuzzleSolver.analyze()`'s existing contract for invalid input.
- Analyzing an unsolvable `PuzzleDefinition` (e.g. two arrows blocking each other in a cycle with no external legal arrow) must report the geometric dependency-graph properties that can still be computed (e.g. edge existence) without claiming a legal-move-structure sequence that doesn't exist, and must not be confused with the solver's own `solvable: false` result.
- A dependency cycle among remaining arrows (geometrically possible even though no catalog puzzle should ship one) must be representable in the graph model and must not cause an infinite loop or unbounded recursion in any depth/chain computation.
- Repeated analysis calls against the same definition, and analysis calls interleaved with real gameplay using a different `PuzzleState` built from the same or a different definition, must never leak metric values or cross-contaminate results between calls.
- The developer report must remain usable and correctly ordered as the catalog grows from eight to fourteen entries, without hardcoding assumptions about exactly eight puzzles.
- None of the six new experimental puzzles may introduce a mechanic, input method, control, or failure state not already used by the existing eight (no lives, timers, hints, special arrow types, or forced restarts), and each must remain playable to completion with the existing scoring/mistakes/accuracy model, including via keyboard/gamepad navigation identical to the existing catalog's play flow.
- If a candidate experimental puzzle, once authored and analyzed, does not actually exhibit its intended structural property (e.g. an intended "cascade" puzzle whose maximum unlock fan-out turns out to be 1), it must be revised before being added to the shipped catalog rather than shipped as a mislabeled experiment.
- The calibration worksheet must remain usable even if a developer skips the optional "frustrating because" field, and must not silently invent or infer challenge/discoverability ratings from objective metrics when a human hasn't recorded them.

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The system MUST provide a headless structural-analysis capability that consumes a `PuzzleDefinition` and returns objective board-scale metrics: board width, board height, total board cells, arrow count, occupied cell count, and density/occupancy ratio, keeping board scale and density independently visible rather than collapsed into one figure.
- **FR-002**: The analysis capability MUST also return arrow-geometry metrics: single-cell arrow count, multi-cell arrow count, average arrow length, maximum arrow length, bent-arrow count, total bend count, average bends per arrow, and maximum bends on any one arrow.
- **FR-003**: The analysis capability MUST also return legal-move-structure metrics, reusing existing `PuzzleSolver` witness data where appropriate: initial legal move count and ratio, minimum/maximum/average legal choices encountered across the witness, forced-state count and ratio, branching-state count and ratio, longest consecutive forced-state run, and the full ordered sequence of legal-choice counts across the witness (not only aggregated sums).
- **FR-004**: The system MUST formalize a dependency-graph representation of the existing blocking relationship — one node per active arrow, one directed edge A→B meaning "a cell A currently occupies lies on B's forward escape ray," i.e. **A blocks B** — as a read-only analytical view. Under this orientation, an arrow's out-degree counts the arrows it blocks and its in-degree counts the arrows currently blocking it. This graph MUST be derived from the same blocking definition `PuzzleState.is_blocked` already uses and MUST NOT introduce a second, independently-implemented rules engine.
- **FR-005**: From the dependency graph, the system MUST measure: initial dependency edge count, dependency depth (longest directed chain), maximum blockers affecting one arrow (maximum in-degree), longest dependency chain, and number of independent dependency chains/components where precisely definable. A static out-degree count from the graph alone MUST NOT be reported or treated as "cascade fan-out": because a blocked arrow (B) may have more than one blocker, removing one of A's blocked neighbors does not guarantee B becomes legal, so cascade/unlock fan-out (FR-006) MUST be measured empirically from the solver's witness, never derived solely from static out-degree.
- **FR-006**: The system MUST measure, per removal step in a puzzle's solver witness, how many previously-blocked arrows become newly legal (i.e. arrows with no remaining active blocker), exposing this as an inspectable sequence so cascade events (fan-out ≥ 2) and their position in the solution sequence can be identified. This is the system's one and only definition of "maximum unlock fan-out."
- **FR-007**: The analysis capability MUST expose geometric blocker-distance proxies (e.g. how far along a blocked arrow's forward ray the blocking cell sits) as explicitly labeled approximations of visual/perceptual difficulty, never presented as a validated measurement of human perception.
- **FR-008**: The analysis capability MUST be deterministic (repeated calls against the same definition return identical results), MUST NOT mutate the supplied `PuzzleDefinition`, MUST NOT read, mutate, or otherwise depend upon any *live* gameplay `PuzzleState` (i.e. one backing an actual in-progress or replayable attempt), MUST NOT read or write any session-scoped current-puzzle state, and MUST work headlessly with no dependency on scenes, Nodes, Controls, or any other presentation concept. The analysis capability MAY internally construct its own private, fresh domain state (e.g. a scratch `PuzzleState`, reused exactly as `PuzzleSolver.analyze()` already does) in order to reuse authoritative gameplay-rule evaluation rather than reimplementing blocking/legality logic — provided that internal state is isolated from any live attempt, deterministic, and discarded once analysis completes, leaving no observable side effect outside the returned result.
- **FR-009**: The system MUST NOT compute, expose, or persist any composite difficulty score, formula, numeric difficulty rating, or Easy/Medium/Hard-style classification anywhere — not in the analysis capability's output contract, not in the puzzle catalog, and not player-facing.
- **FR-010**: The system MUST NOT alter `PuzzleSolver`'s existing correctness behavior, its existing `analyze()` return contract (`valid`, `solvable`, `witness`, `validation_errors`, `metrics`), or any of its currently-defined metric semantics; any new metrics MUST be additive, whether exposed via `PuzzleSolver` itself or an adjacent analysis component, and MUST NOT be used to alter which puzzles the solver considers solvable.
- **FR-011**: `PuzzleDefinition` MUST NOT gain any new fields as a result of this spec (no difficulty tags, no analysis-cache fields, no catalog/progression fields); it remains anonymous structural puzzle data exactly as today.
- **FR-012**: The system MUST provide six new experimental authored puzzles, added to `PuzzleCatalog` alongside the existing eight (bringing the catalog to fourteen total), each with a stable ID independent of array position, a short display title, and deterministic catalog order, following the same authoring precedent (plain GDScript-constructed `PuzzleDefinition`, no `.tres`/JSON/scene-per-puzzle format) as the existing eight. A single puzzle MAY satisfy more than one of the named-experiment requirements below (FR-013 through FR-018) simultaneously; such overlap is desirable evidence about which structural combinations are naturally compatible, not a violation of any individual requirement, and no puzzle is restricted to exactly one hypothesis.
- **FR-013**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST deliberately exercise a multi-step dependency chain whose solution sequence is spatially/visually distributed (not laid out as one obvious row or pattern), combining dependency depth with spatial separation and, where practical, bends and blockers in different board regions ("Nested Chain").
- **FR-014**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST deliberately produce a measurable cascade: removing one specific arrow MUST cause at least two other previously-blocked arrows to become legal in the same step, verifiable via FR-006's per-step unlock measurement ("Cascade / Key Arrow").
- **FR-015**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST combine meaningful board occupancy/density with real, non-trivial dependency depth — explicitly not the existing catalog's pattern where a dense-looking board is nearly all immediately legal ("Dense Unravel").
- **FR-016**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST contain multiple bent arrows whose geometry meaningfully participates in the dependency graph (i.e. at least one dependency edge involves a bent arrow's tail cell as the blocking or blocked geometry, not merely decorative bends with no dependency role) ("Bent Network").
- **FR-017**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST contain at least one arrow that is blocked by a cell meaningfully far along its forward escape ray on a board large enough for that distance to be non-trivial, preferably involving a blocker that itself belongs to a multi-cell arrow rather than only an isolated single-cell blocker ("Long-Range Blocker").
- **FR-018**: At least one of the six new puzzles MUST be authored on a board large enough that its occupied cells form an intentional, recognizable macro-level composition/shape, while its arrows still participate in at least one genuine dependency edge — the composition MUST emerge from ordinary puzzle cells and MUST NOT require any new rendering mechanic ("Composed / Shaped").
- **FR-019**: The system MUST provide a developer-only occupancy/grid visualization capability sufficient to inspect a puzzle's macro shape, density, and symmetry for authoring and review purposes. This MUST NOT replace or extend `PuzzleDefinition` as an authoring format and MUST NOT introduce a second puzzle-authoring language.
- **FR-020**: The existing automated content-regression gate (currently covering eight puzzles) MUST be extended, not duplicated, to cover all fourteen catalog puzzles, verifying for every entry: unique stable ID, structural validity, solver-confirmed solvability, a complete witness, and that replaying that witness against a fresh `PuzzleState` clears the puzzle with zero mistakes.
- **FR-021**: The system MUST provide a deterministic, developer-facing report capable of comparing the full puzzle catalog and answering at minimum: which puzzle has the deepest dependency chain; which begins with the fewest legal arrows; which has the widest branching; which produces the largest cascade; which has the longest forced run; which has the highest density; which contains the most bends; which has the longest blocker distance; which has the largest board; and how the legal-choice count changes throughout each puzzle's witness. This report MUST NOT be a new in-game analytics screen.
- **FR-022**: The system MUST provide a lightweight manual human-calibration worksheet/process, completable in on the order of seconds per puzzle after playing it, recording per puzzle: puzzle ID, score, mistakes, accuracy (from existing gameplay results); perceived challenge (1–5); scanning load (low/medium/high); sequence discoverability (obvious / required some tracing / required significant tracing); whether an aha/unlock moment occurred (yes/no); whether the puzzle felt solved before mechanical completion (yes/no); and a short free-text note on what was interesting (with an optional free-text note on what was frustrating). This worksheet MUST introduce no telemetry, no persistence infrastructure, and no analytics service.
- **FR-023**: This spec MUST NOT introduce procedural or randomized puzzle generation of any kind, a composite difficulty formula or score, any player-facing difficulty rating or classification (Easy/Medium/Hard, stars, or equivalent), persistent puzzle progression (unlocks, completion state, best score/accuracy persistence, achievements, campaign completion), telemetry, remote analytics, new gameplay mechanics (lives, timers, hints, power-ups, special arrow types), or any change to existing gameplay rules, scoring, rendering, departure, navigation, or session-selection behavior.

- **FR-025**: Implementation MUST update affected durable architecture/product knowledge to describe the analysis capability's boundaries and metric definitions, the dependency-graph interpretation, the distinction between objective metrics and labeled perceptual hypotheses (dependency discoverability, false affordance, unlock rhythm, reasoning span, composition), and the enlarged puzzle catalog — without durable backlinks to this temporary spec bundle, and without recording any hypothesis as established product truth before evidence supports it.
- **FR-026**: After all six experimental puzzles' human-calibration worksheets (FR-022) are complete, the system MUST produce a short developer-facing findings summary that states, per named experiment (Nested Chain, Cascade/Key Arrow, Dense Unravel, Bent Network, Long-Range Blocker, Composed/Shaped), whether the structural hypothesis it targeted was supported, contradicted, or remains inconclusive by the calibration data, and names which structural characteristics warrant further investigation before any future puzzle-generation spec. This summary MUST NOT assert a composite difficulty score or formula, MUST NOT propose or sketch a generator design, and MUST NOT be recorded as established product truth in durable knowledge — it is this spec's evidence handoff to whatever specification follows, not a conclusion baked into the game.

### Key Entities

- **Puzzle Structural Analysis**: A read-only, deterministic capability that consumes a `PuzzleDefinition` (and, where useful, an existing `PuzzleSolver` witness/metrics result) and produces objective structural measurements — board scale, density, arrow geometry, legal-move structure, dependency-graph properties, cascade/unlock-fan-out, and blocker-distance proxies. Owns no gameplay rules, no mutable state, and no difficulty score.
- **Dependency Graph**: An analytical, monotonically-shrinking directed graph over currently-active arrows, where an edge A→B means A currently occupies a cell on B's forward escape ray, derived from the exact blocking definition `PuzzleState.is_blocked` already implements. Never a second rules authority; never mutated independently of the puzzle it describes.
- **Experimental Puzzle**: One of six new `PuzzleCatalog` entries, each authored to deliberately exercise a named, previously-untested structural combination (nested chain, cascade/key arrow, dense unravel, bent network, long-range blocker, composed/shaped) rather than a generic difficulty increase.
- **Developer Structural Report**: A deterministic, developer-facing comparison across the full puzzle catalog exposing the metrics above per puzzle, for authoring and evaluation use only — never an in-game or player-facing surface.
- **Human Calibration Record**: A lightweight, non-persistent, per-puzzle worksheet entry pairing existing objective gameplay results (score, mistakes, accuracy) with a short set of human observations (challenge rating, scanning load, discoverability, aha-moment flag, early-completion flag, free-text interest/frustration notes), used only to compare against structural metrics — never telemetry, never an account-linked record.
- **Findings Summary**: A short, developer-facing, non-durable document produced once all six calibration records are complete, stating per-experiment agreement/contradiction/inconclusive status against the targeted hypothesis and flagging characteristics for further investigation. This is this spec's evidence handoff to a future specification, not a durable knowledge artifact, a difficulty score, or a generator design.

## Success Criteria

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Any valid `PuzzleDefinition` — including at least ten small synthetic test puzzles with hand-computed expected values covering independent arrows, simple and deep chains, branching, cascades, multiple blockers on one arrow, bent-arrow dependencies, long-range blockers, and invalid input — can be analyzed headlessly, with 100% of those expected values matched exactly.
- **SC-002**: Repeated analysis of the same puzzle definition produces identical results in 100% of tested cases, and analysis never mutates the supplied definition, any gameplay state, or any session state, verified by automated tests.
- **SC-003**: All fourteen catalog puzzles (eight existing, six new) are structurally valid and solver-confirmed solvable, each with a witness that replays against a fresh `PuzzleState` with zero mistakes, verified by the extended automated content-regression gate with zero manual steps.
- **SC-004**: Each of the six new experimental puzzles measurably exhibits its named structural property via the analyzer's own output (e.g. the cascade puzzle shows unlock fan-out ≥ 2; the bent-network puzzle shows a dependency edge involving a bent arrow's tail; the long-range-blocker puzzle shows a blocker distance clearly beyond immediate adjacency; the composed/shaped puzzle's occupied cells form a recognizable macro shape while containing at least one genuine dependency edge) — 100% of the six meet their defining property before being considered complete.
- **SC-005**: A developer can answer all nine catalog-comparison questions listed in FR-021 by reading the developer report's output alone, without manual recomputation, for the full fourteen-puzzle catalog.
- **SC-006**: The human-calibration worksheet is completed for all six new experimental puzzles, with each entry taking on the order of seconds to fill out and requiring no telemetry, account, or persistence infrastructure.
- **SC-007**: Every existing spec 001–005 behavior (domain rules, solver correctness/contract, scoring, continuous rendering, path-following departure, hover/blocked feedback, Level Select, Replay, Next Puzzle, session-only selection, no-reset-on-entry guarantee) is unchanged when exercised through any of the fourteen catalog puzzles, verified by the existing regression suites continuing to pass alongside new coverage.
- **SC-008**: No composite difficulty score, numeric difficulty rating, or Easy/Medium/Hard-style classification exists anywhere in the shipped system as a result of this spec, verified by review of the analysis capability's output contract, the catalog, and all player-facing surfaces.
- **SC-009**: No procedural or randomized puzzle generation exists anywhere in the shipped system as a result of this spec, verified by review of the puzzle catalog and analysis capability.
- **SC-010**: A findings summary (FR-026) is produced after all six calibration records are complete, stating a supported/contradicted/inconclusive verdict for each of the six named experiments and naming at least one characteristic for further investigation, without asserting a composite difficulty score, formula, or generator design.
