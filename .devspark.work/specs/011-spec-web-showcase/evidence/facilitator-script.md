# Facilitator script: observed fresh-player sessions

For genuinely new players: people who have not seen the game, the story or the design documents before the session. Raw notes stay **private, outside any repository**, and are deleted after the closeout. The repository receives one anonymized summary per session (`sessions/NN.md`, from `sessions/_summary-template.md`).

## Before the session

1. Ask: "Have you seen ArrowSpark, its website or any of its write-ups before?" If yes, the session can still count for the DevSpark comprehension question, but not as a fresh player.
2. **Consent line (say it):** "Anonymized quotes from this session may be published as part of the project write-up. I won't record your name, contact details or employer. If you'd rather not be quoted, I'll only paraphrase you." Note the answer (quote allowed / paraphrase only).
3. Open `https://arrow.makeboldspark.com` on a desktop browser with a mouse or trackpad. Have a clock ready.

## Order (never change it; record any deviation)

1. **Landing impression.** Show the landing page. Ask only: "What is this page offering you? Does either option look more important than the other?" (equal billing). Then: "Whose site does this look like, and what kind of site?" (theme recognition).
2. **Fresh play.** Say only: **"Here's a puzzle game. Play it however you like."** Start the clock when they click into the game. Observe silently. Stop when they finish, stop on their own, or after 30 minutes.
3. **Fresh-play interview.** One question at a time, in this order; write their words as given, including negative ones.
4. **Built with DevSpark.** Say: "There's a second half to this site about how it was built. Have a look at it however you like." Let them read for as long as they want.
5. **DevSpark comprehension, then reaction.** Ask the three comprehension questions, then invite (don't push) the optional reaction forms.

## What not to say or do

- No design vocabulary of any kind (the words used to design the level, or descriptions of what the level contains).
- No coaching, hints, or "try zooming". If asked how something works, say "Do whatever you think" and note that they asked.
- Don't explain Level Select, the groups, or which puzzle is "the" level.
- Don't react to mistakes or praise moves. Note anything you did that could have guided them.

## Facilitator checklist (observe during play; ask in the interview what you couldn't see)

| # | Question | Observe or ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did they start without guidance? | observe |
| 2 | Did they understand what to do? | observe, then ask "What did you think you were supposed to do?" |
| 3 | Could they trace arrows? | observe |
| 4 | Did they zoom or pan unprompted? | observe |
| 5 | Did areas of the board start to feel separate as they played? (their words, never ours) | ask "Did the board change how it looked to you as you went?" |
| 6 | Did they have short "I can see the next few moves" moments? | ask "Were there moments you could see what was coming?" |
| 7 | Did big clearances feel satisfying? | ask "Was any moment especially satisfying?" |
| 8 | Did any stretch become mindless clicking? | ask "Did any part feel like just clicking?" |
| 9 | Did they use Show Me an Open Move? | observe |
| 10 | Did they understand its cost? | ask "What did that button cost you, if anything?" |
| 11 | Did the ending stay interesting? | ask "How did the end feel?" |
| 12 | Would they play another one like it? | ask |
| 13 | Would they recommend it? | ask |
| 14 | **Discovery:** did they open Level Select and find the ArrowSpark Levels group, and how long did it take? | observe; record time from first opening the main menu and the entry they started from |

Also record from the Results screen: completed?, mistakes, Open Move assists, score; your clock time; and the puzzle content version **`g1-7ce0942d4a5e`** (copy it from the Evidence page; never read it from the game).

## DevSpark comprehension (after they've read Built with DevSpark)

Ask one at a time, write the answers verbatim:

1. "In your own words, what is DevSpark?"
2. "Where did it help, in this project?"
3. "Where did it fail or get in the way?"

Afterwards note, for each answer, where it matched or missed what the story intended.
